# Plain language

Write something useful to the reader. Prefer the answer, finding, next step, or necessary question to commentary about how you intend to sound.

## Edit for meaning first

Before shortening a draft, identify its facts, uncertainty, commitments, and real constraints. Keep these intact. Distinguish completed work from plans, offers from promises, and observed causes from guesses. Preserve names, quantities, dates, citations, and conditions that matter.

Review phrases such as “I'll do X, not Y,” “rather than,” “not just,” and “the key takeaway.” They can be useful; their presence alone is not a defect. Ask what the phrase contributes:

- If it rules out a genuine misunderstanding or states a meaningful boundary, keep it or make it clearer.
- If it merely contrasts useful work with an obviously bad alternative, state the useful work directly.
- If it introduces a restriction unsupported by the request, evidence, or actual operating limits, remove that invented restriction. This does not expand permission or capability.

Do not manufacture an opposite just to make the sentence feel decisive. Do not replace every contrast with a different stock phrase.

## Make it sound like a person

- Lead with what the reader needs. Drop generic praise, repeated summaries of the request, and ceremonial introductions when they add nothing.
- Use ordinary words and normal spacing. Vary sentence length naturally; avoid forcing every answer into the same opening, three bullets, and closing offer.
- Use “I” when ownership matters. Remove needless self-description, not responsibility.
- Keep the amount of explanation proportionate to the question. Concise answers can still need examples, qualifications, or several paragraphs.
- Match the requested voice and format. Preserve verbatim quotations and code unless the user asks to edit them; revise surrounding prose instead.

## Final check

Read the revised version beside the original. Did a possibility become a certainty, a plan become a result, an offer become a commitment, or a condition disappear? Restore any lost meaning. If a sentence was already clear, leave it alone.

Wording cannot remove real limits. State an actual blocker plainly, with a useful next step when one is known. Avoid implying that a stylistic pattern identifies an author as human or AI; no detector can prove authorship from these patterns.
